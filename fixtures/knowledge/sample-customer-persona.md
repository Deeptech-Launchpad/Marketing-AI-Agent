# Meridian Catalog Services — Buyer Personas (SYNTHETIC)

## Primary persona — "Dana", Ecommerce Manager

**Role and context.** Dana runs the webshop for an industrial distributor
carrying roughly 18,000 SKUs across 200-plus manufacturers. She reports to a
commercial director who measures her on online revenue and on the ratio of
online to phone orders. She has one developer, shared with another department.

**What her day looks like.** Chasing supplier feeds that arrive in inconsistent
formats. Fixing category assignments by hand. Fielding requests from the sales
desk to "get the spec up" for products a customer has just asked about.

**Her pains, in her own invented words.**
- "Half my catalog is a title and a price."
- "The sales desk still gets calls asking for things that are on the datasheet."
- "I know our filters are useless but I don't have the data to make them work."
- "Every supplier sends a different spreadsheet and none of them map cleanly."

**What triggers her to look for help.** A replatforming project. A new product
line from a manufacturer with better data, showing up the rest of the catalog.
A competitor's site appearing above hers on an attribute-led search.

**What she is sceptical about.** Agencies that promise ranking improvements.
Anything that requires her one developer for weeks. Tools that produce a CSV she
then has to import herself.

**What convinces her.** Seeing five of her own SKUs enriched, side by side with
the originals. A clear statement of what will be delivered per SKU. Evidence the
work happens against her platform rather than in a spreadsheet.

**Channels she actually uses.** Email, which she reads early. LinkedIn, browsed
rather than posted to. Industry trade publications occasionally.

## Secondary persona — "Marcus", Operations Director

**Role and context.** Marcus owns the P&L for the distribution business. He does
not care about product data as a topic. He cares about order accuracy, return
rates, and the cost of the inside sales team.

**His pains.** Returns caused by fitment errors. Inside sales spending its day on
specification lookups instead of selling. No visibility into which catalog gaps
cost the most.

**What convinces him.** Cost avoided, expressed in headcount time. A defined
scope with a defined end. Not a subscription.

**What loses him.** Anything framed as a marketing initiative. Percentage claims
he cannot verify.

## Anti-persona — who Meridian should not target

**"Priya", Brand Marketing Manager at a consumer retailer.** Shallow catalog,
lifestyle-led merchandising, attributes largely irrelevant to purchase. Meridian
has nothing useful to sell her, and content aimed at Dana reads as noise to her.

**Single-brand manufacturers.** They already own their product data and have no
supplier-feed fragmentation problem.

## Buying process (invented)

Dana identifies the need and builds the internal case. Marcus approves spend
above a threshold. Procurement is involved only above a larger threshold. The
cycle in this invented model runs six to fourteen weeks, and stalls most often
between Dana's interest and Marcus's approval — which is why material aimed at
Dana should give her something she can forward to Marcus without rewriting it.
