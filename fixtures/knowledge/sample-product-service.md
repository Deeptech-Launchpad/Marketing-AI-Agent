# Meridian Catalog Services — Service Lines (SYNTHETIC)

## Overview

Meridian sells five service lines. All five operate on the same underlying
asset: the customer's product catalog. They are sold individually or as a
catalog-wide programme.

## Service line 1 — Technical specification enrichment

The core offering. Meridian sources missing product attributes and writes them
back into the customer's product records in a structured, filterable form.

Attributes covered in this invented scope include physical dimensions, weight,
material composition, load and pressure ratings, operating temperature ranges,
electrical characteristics, thread and connection standards, compliance and
certification markings, and cross-manufacturer compatibility or fitment data.

The problem this addresses: industrial buyers filter and compare on attributes.
A product record that lacks the attribute a buyer is filtering on is excluded
from the result set before any human reads the description. The product does not
lose on price or availability. It is never seen.

## Service line 2 — Product documentation sourcing

Meridian locates and attaches the manufacturer material that belongs with a
product record but is usually missing from it: specification sheets, installation
and maintenance manuals, CAD and BIM files, exploded parts diagrams, warranty
statements, and safety data sheets.

The problem this addresses: for a distributor carrying products from several
hundred manufacturers, this material almost always exists somewhere, but nobody
owns the job of connecting it to the SKU. Buyers who cannot find a datasheet
either call, or buy elsewhere.

## Service line 3 — Product imagery enhancement

Multi-angle photography, scale and context images, technical line drawings, and
annotated detail shots highlighting the feature a buyer is actually evaluating.

The problem this addresses: technical products are frequently listed with a
single low-resolution manufacturer stock image that does not show the mounting
face, the connection type, or the finish. Buyers cannot confirm fit from the
image, so they hesitate.

## Service line 4 — SEO-structured copy and taxonomy

Product copy written against the vocabulary buyers actually search, structured
attribute schemas, consistent category taxonomies, and structured data markup so
attributes are machine readable by search engines and internal site search.

The problem this addresses: catalogs assembled from supplier feeds inherit each
supplier's naming conventions. The same fitting appears under four different
category names, and internal search returns nothing useful.

## Service line 5 — Bulk catalog migration and enrichment

Catalog-wide programmes sized to the customer's platform. Import mechanics differ
substantially between Magento, BigCommerce, Shopify and WooCommerce, and the
approach is set per platform rather than applied generically.

The problem this addresses: catalogs that grew through acquisition or supplier
consolidation contain overlapping, contradictory and duplicated records, and no
single person understands the whole of it.

## Who these services are for

Industrial and B2B distributors with deep technical catalogs. In this invented
profile, the typical customer carries between 2,000 and 40,000 SKUs, sells to
trade rather than consumers, and runs Magento or BigCommerce. The recurring
pattern is a catalog that grew faster than anyone's ability to maintain it, where
data quality varies by supplier and ownership is unclear.

## Who these services are not for

Consumer retail catalogs with shallow attribute requirements. Single-brand
manufacturers who already control their own product data. Marketplaces that
receive data from sellers rather than owning it.

## Commercial model

Priced per SKU per attribute depth, with a minimum engagement size. Delivered in
batches. No software licence, no ongoing platform fee, no per-seat pricing in
this invented model.

## Claims discipline

Meridian does not publish percentage improvement figures for conversion, revenue
or search ranking. Customers in this invented scenario report qualitative
outcomes: fewer inbound calls asking for published specifications, faster
time-to-publish for new SKUs, and fewer returns caused by fitment errors.
Quantified claims are only made where a specific named customer has agreed to
them in writing.
