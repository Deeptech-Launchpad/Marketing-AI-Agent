# Meridian Catalog Services — Brand & Tone Guidelines (SYNTHETIC)

## Tone of voice

Meridian writes the way a competent technical peer speaks. Direct, specific, and
unhurried. The reader is assumed to be an operations or ecommerce manager who
knows their catalog better than we do.

**Write like this:** plain declarative sentences. Concrete nouns. The specific
attribute, not "product data". The named platform, not "your ecommerce system".

**Do not write like this:** breathless, urgent, or salesy. No manufactured
scarcity. No rhetorical questions used as openers.

## Required characteristics

- Lead with the reader's situation, not with who we are.
- Name a specific, observable problem within the first two sentences.
- Prefer the concrete example over the general category.
- Short paragraphs. No paragraph longer than four sentences.
- British or American spelling is acceptable, but be consistent within a piece.

## Banned language

The following must never appear in Meridian marketing content:

- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class",
  "world-class", "industry-leading", "next-generation", "seamless", "synergy".
- "Unlock", "supercharge", "turbocharge", "skyrocket", "10x".
- Any superlative about Meridian's own position: "the leading", "the number one",
  "the most trusted".
- Emoji in any professional channel.
- Exclamation marks outside of direct quotations.

## Claim substantiation rules

This is the strictest rule in the guidelines and overrides tone preferences.

- **No percentage figures** for conversion, revenue, traffic, ranking or time
  saved, unless the specific figure appears in a signed, named case study.
- **No guarantees** of any outcome. Meridian does not guarantee results.
- **No comparative claims** against named or unnamed competitors.
- **No customer names** may be used without written permission recorded in the
  case study document itself.
- Where a claim cannot be substantiated, state the mechanism instead of the
  outcome. "Complete attribute data means the product appears in filtered
  results" is permitted. "Complete attribute data increases sales by 30%" is not.

## Calls to action

Preferred: an offer of a specific, small, concrete next step.

- "Book a 15-minute catalog review"
- "See a sample enriched product record"
- "Send us five SKUs and we will show you what is missing"

Avoid: "Learn more", "Get started today", "Don't miss out", "Contact us now".

## Formatting conventions

- Sentence case for headlines, not Title Case.
- Product and platform names capitalised exactly as their owners write them:
  Magento, BigCommerce, WooCommerce, Shopify.
- "SKU" and "PIM" may be used without expansion. "PDP" must be expanded on first
  use as "product detail page".
- Numbers under ten written as words, except measurements and SKU counts.

## Channel notes

**Email** — a single idea per message. No more than 150 words in the body. One
call to action, placed at the end.

**LinkedIn** — the first two lines must stand alone, because everything after
them is hidden behind "see more". No hashtag walls; at most two, and only where
they are genuine industry terms.

## Things Meridian never does

- Cold-calls a contact who has asked not to be contacted.
- Uses a customer's logo before the case study is signed off.
- Publishes benchmark data sourced from a competitor's marketing material.
